Classical electrodynamics in the form molecular-property theory uses: Maxwell's equations and potentials, the coupling of charged particles to fields, light as a plane wave, multipole expansions, and fields in matter. The source is Chapter 3 of Norman, Ruud and Saue, *Principles and Practices of Molecular Properties*.

## What this chapter is about

A molecular property is the response of a molecule to an electric or magnetic field. This chapter sets up everything on the field side of that statement:

- what the fields are and how they are generated;
- how they enter a Hamiltonian;
- how they are expanded around a molecule;
- how the response of many molecules turns into the macroscopic permittivity, refractive index and absorption coefficient that are measured.

## Why it matters

- **Minimal coupling and the Pauli term** are the starting point of every magnetic property and of the relativistic Hamiltonians in Part 4.
- **The multipolar gauge and the electric dipole approximation** justify the $-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf E$ perturbation used for polarizabilities and spectra. They also show when it fails (X-rays, large molecules).
- **The origin dependence of multipoles** explains why only the lowest nonvanishing moment is origin independent.
- **The local field and Clausius–Mossotti/Onsager** connect computed $\alpha$ and $\mu$ to measured $\varepsilon$ and $n$. Solvation models start here.
- **Kramers–Kronig and the Debye and Lorentz models** are the classical template of damped response functions.

## The main ideas, in order

1. **Maxwell's equations and their structure.** Continuity, the Helmholtz decomposition into longitudinal and transverse parts, retarded solutions, and the nonrelativistic limit.
2. **Potentials and gauges.** Gauge freedom, the Lorenz and Coulomb gauges, and the covariant field tensor and Lagrangian.
3. **Charged particles in fields.** The Lorentz force from minimal coupling, the Poynting theorem, the Pauli term, and charge and current densities.
4. **Plane waves.** Polarization, handedness, and intensity.
5. **The multipolar gauge.** The multipole interaction series and the electric dipole approximation.
6. **Multipole moments.** Cartesian and traceless forms, origin shifts, parity, and magnetic moments.
7. **Matter.** $\mathbf P$, $\mathbf M$, $\mathbf D$, $\mathbf H$, constitutive relations, and boundary conditions.
8. **From molecules to media.** The internal field, Clausius–Mossotti and Lorentz–Lorenz, the Debye equation, Onsager's reaction field, and the Kirkwood factor.
9. **Waves in media.** The complex refractive index, the Beer–Lambert law, and cross sections.
10. **Frequency dependence.** Causality, Kramers–Kronig, Debye relaxation, Lorentz oscillators, and the plasma limit.
11. **Units and magnitudes.** Dimensions, atomic units of fields and properties, and esu conversion.
