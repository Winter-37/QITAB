Quantum information and quantum computing: the background needed to read the quantum-chemistry algorithms of Part 6. The main source is Nielsen–Chuang.

## What this part is about

A quantum computer stores information in qubits, processes it with unitary gates, and reads it out by measurement. This part builds that picture from the postulates of quantum mechanics:

- information-theoretic foundations (Chapter 1);
- qubits and their states (Chapter 2);
- gates (Chapter 3) and circuits (Chapter 4);
- the algorithms that show a quantum advantage (Chapter 5);
- the practical limits: error correction, hardware and complexity (Chapters 6–8);
- variational algorithms and software (Chapters 9–11).

## Why it is here

Part 6 maps the electronic Hamiltonian to qubits and solves it with VQE, phase estimation or sampling. Reading those notes needs a working knowledge of several ideas from this part:

- what a gate and a circuit are;
- why measurement gives samples rather than amplitudes;
- how phase estimation extracts an eigenvalue;
- what noise does to a computation.

Most of the mathematics is already in Parts 0 and 2: tensor products, density operators, spin ½ and SU(2). This part reuses it in a new setting.
