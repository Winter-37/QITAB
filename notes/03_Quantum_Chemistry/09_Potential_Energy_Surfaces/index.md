Potential energy surfaces as chemical objects: minima, transition states, reaction paths and conical intersections, and how their features determine structure and reactivity. Computing energies, gradients and Hessians on the surface is covered in Part 4, Chapters 15-16; this chapter is about what to do with them.

## What this chapter is about

- **Stationary points.** Minima (conformers, intermediates) and saddle points, classified by the Hessian.
- **Reaction paths.** The intrinsic reaction coordinate in mass-weighted coordinates, and what a barrier height does and does not tell about a rate.

## Why it matters

Mechanisms are read off a surface: which minima exist, which saddle points connect them, and how high those saddles are. Every optimized structure needs a frequency check before it can be called a minimum or a transition state.
