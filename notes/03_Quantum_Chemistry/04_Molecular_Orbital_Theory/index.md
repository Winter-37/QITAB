Qualitative and semi-quantitative molecular-orbital theory: the language chemists use to reason about bonding, spectra and stability, checked against ab initio calculations.

## What this chapter is about

Molecular orbitals are built from atomic orbitals by symmetry and energy matching, then filled like atomic shells. With a handful of rules this predicts which diatomics are bound and paramagnetic, which terms a configuration gives, how electrons are distributed in polar molecules, and which ring systems are aromatic:

- LCAO molecular orbitals of homonuclear and heteronuclear diatomics, the correlation diagram, bond orders;
- term symbols of diatomic states;
- symmetry orbitals and the canonical MOs of polyatomic molecules (water, methane, ethylene);
- Hückel theory of conjugated $\pi$ systems.

## Why it matters

- **It is the vocabulary of the rest of the notebook.** Configurations such as $1\pi_g^2$, labels such as $3a_1$ or $1t_2$, and the bonding/antibonding distinction appear in every chapter of Part 4.
- **Its limits are instructive.** Hartree–Fock orbital energies give the wrong $\sigma$/$\pi$ order for N₂ and F₂, near-HF dipoles give CO the wrong polarity, and Hückel's $\beta$ depends on the property it is fitted to. Each failure points to the correlated methods of Part 4.

## The main ideas, in order

1. **Diatomic MOs.** $\sigma$/$\pi$, g/u, bonding/antibonding; noncrossing rule; filling order; bond order and its correlation with $D_e$ and $R_e$; the $\sigma_g2p$/$\pi_u2p$ switch.
2. **Terms.** $\Lambda$, $S$, $\Sigma^\pm$, g/u from configurations; spin–orbit levels; the low-lying states of O₂.
3. **Heteronuclear diatomics.** The two-AO secular equation; CO, BF, HF; polarity and dipole moments.
4. **Polyatomic MOs.** Symmetry orbitals block-diagonalize the Roothaan equations; the MOs of H₂O and CH₄.
5. **Hückel theory.** Adjacency-matrix eigenvalues; polyenes, annulenes and the 4m+2 rule; alternant pairing; bond orders and bond lengths.
