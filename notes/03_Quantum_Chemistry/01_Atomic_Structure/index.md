Many-electron atoms as chemistry sees them: shell structure, electron configurations, screening, term symbols and spin–orbit coupling, ionization energies and periodic trends.

## What this chapter is about

Part 2, Chapter 5 sets out the quantum mechanics of identical particles and the periodic table. This chapter applies it to real atoms with explicit calculations: how the Pauli principle forces shell structure, and how far simple models (unscreened orbitals plus perturbation theory, screened orbitals in one determinant) get towards the true energy. It also shows what is left for electron correlation.

## Why it matters

- **Screening.** Effective nuclear charges, the 2s/2p splitting and the ordering of shells are the first approximations every chemist uses. Here they are derived rather than quoted.
- **The single-determinant limit.** Even the best orbitals in one determinant miss part of the energy. The size of that gap, the correlation energy, sets the scale for the correlated methods of Part 4.
