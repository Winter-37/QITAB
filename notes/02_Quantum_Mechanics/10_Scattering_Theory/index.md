Scattering in three dimensions: how a beam of particles is deflected by a localized potential, and how the measured cross sections are computed from the Schrödinger equation. Scattering is the continuum counterpart of the bound-state problem and the main experimental window on interactions.

**Path.** [[scattering-cross-sections-and-amplitude]] sets up cross sections and the scattering amplitude. [[partial-wave-analysis-and-phase-shifts]] gives the exact solution for central potentials in terms of phase shifts, with unitarity and the optical theorem. [[born-approximation-and-lippmann-schwinger]] gives the integral equation, the Born approximation and its range of validity. [[low-energy-scattering-and-resonances]] treats the scattering length, the link to bound states, and resonances.

**Needs.** Chapter 4 (central potentials, spherical harmonics, angular momentum); one-dimensional scattering and the finite well from Chapter 2; the single-particle Green's function from Chapter 8. Chapter 9 (WKB) is used for the tunnelling picture of resonance widths.

**Leads to.** The golden rule of Chapter 11 ([[fermi-golden-rule-and-decay-width]]) gives the Born cross section by another route. Green's functions and propagators return in Part 4, Chapter 17.

**Planned sections:** identical-particle scattering; the eikonal approximation; Coulomb scattering treated exactly.
