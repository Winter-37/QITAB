Central potentials and spherical harmonics lead to the hydrogen atom, the template for atomic orbitals; then orbital angular momentum, spin and the addition of angular momenta (Griffiths Ch. 4).

## What this chapter is about

In three dimensions a potential that depends only on $r$ separates in spherical coordinates. Every central potential gives the same angular solutions, the spherical harmonics $Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)$, and a one-dimensional radial equation with an extra centrifugal term $\hbar^2 l(l+1)/2mr^2$. Solving that radial equation for the Coulomb potential gives the hydrogen atom.

The same angular structure then appears without any reference to coordinates. Angular momentum is defined by its commutation relations alone, and those relations allow half-integer values. These describe spin, an intrinsic angular momentum with no classical counterpart. The chapter also shows how two angular momenta combine, and how electromagnetic fields enter through the potentials.

## Why it matters

- **Hydrogen is the origin of orbital language.** The quantum numbers $n,l,m$, the shapes of $s,p,d$ orbitals, the $n^2$ degeneracy and the Bohr energies $-13.6\text{ eV}/n^2$ all come from here. Slater-type basis functions and the radial factors of Gaussian basis sets descend from hydrogenic functions.
- **Angular momentum algebra is used everywhere.** Ladder operators, $\hat L^2$ and $\hat L_z$ eigenvalues, and the coupling of two angular momenta into a total are needed for term symbols, spin states and selection rules.
- **Spin is what makes chemistry fermionic.** Two-component spinors, the Pauli matrices and the singlet–triplet construction underlie spin orbitals, Slater determinants and spin contamination in Part 4. The Pauli matrices are also the one-qubit operators of Part 5.
- **Fields enter through potentials.** Minimal coupling $\hat{\mathbf p}\to\hat{\mathbf p}-q\mathbf A$ is the starting point for magnetic properties, the Zeeman effect and gauge questions.

## The main ideas, in order

1. **Three dimensions.** The 3D Schrödinger equation, the canonical commutators, and separation in Cartesian coordinates.
2. **Central potentials.** Separation in spherical coordinates gives spherical harmonics and the radial equation.
3. **Hydrogen.** A power series in the radial equation must terminate, and that gives the Bohr formula, the Bohr radius and radial functions built from associated Laguerre polynomials.
4. **Angular momentum from algebra.** Commutation relations and ladder operators give $l(l+1)\hbar^2$ and $m\hbar$, with half-integer values allowed.
5. **Spin ½.** Spinors, the Pauli matrices, measurement along any axis, and precession in a magnetic field.
6. **Adding angular momenta.** The total runs from $j_1+j_2$ down to $|j_1-j_2|$. For two spins ½ this gives the singlet and triplet, and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients in general.
7. **Electromagnetism.** Minimal coupling, gauge invariance, Landau levels and the Aharonov–Bohm effect.
