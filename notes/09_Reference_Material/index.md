Look-up material rather than reading material: notation, units, identities, convention comparisons and study guides.

## What this part is about

Quantum chemistry texts disagree on notation and conventions. Examples include chemists' versus physicists' integrals, the normalization of RDMs, and the sign of the Fourier transform. This part gathers in one place the material a reader looks up rather than reads:

- the notation used across the notebook;
- units and constants;
- frequently used identities;
- side-by-side comparisons of conventions;
- guides to reading orders.

## Why it is here

Errors of a factor of two or a sign usually come from mixing conventions. A single reference reduces that risk. Material that is first needed in a specific chapter stays there; for example, atomic units are in Part 4, Chapter 1. This part points to it.
